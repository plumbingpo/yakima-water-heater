With an electric water heater, the symptom is often a strong clue. No hot water anywhere suggests a tripped breaker, a tripped high-limit reset, or a failed upper element or upper thermostat, because the upper side controls when the lower side gets power. A few minutes of hot water followed by lukewarm points to the lower element or lower thermostat. Water that comes out scalding points to a thermostat stuck closed or an element shorted to the tank, which is also what trips the red reset button. Catching a symptom early is often the difference between a quick part swap and a tank that gives out on the garage floor. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • Crackling noises as the heater runs, often from sediment
  • A tank that has stopped heating completely
  • A heater that works after a reset, then quits again
  • Scorch marks, melted insulation, or a hot smell at the wiring
  • Dangerously hot water, a sign a thermostat may be stuck
  • A shower that turns lukewarm much sooner than it used to
  • A circuit breaker for the heater that will not stay on
  • Rusty water, leaks around an element, or water at the base

How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Here are our recommendations:

  • Keep the thermostats set around 120 degrees to save energy and reduce scalding risk
  • Protect your elements from hard water with a softener or upgraded low-watt-density elements
  • Never turn the breaker back on until the tank is completely full of water
  • Replace a worn anode rod before corrosion moves on to the tank itself
  • Test the temperature and pressure relief valve once a year
  • Get a diagnosis for any reset button that trips more than once
  • Flush sediment from the tank once or twice a year so it cannot bury the lower element
