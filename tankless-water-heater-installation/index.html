A tankless unit rethinks the whole idea of a water heater. There is no tank to keep hot, so there is no standby loss while you sleep or travel. When a faucet or shower opens, the unit senses flow and fires, heating water as it moves through a heat exchanger. The result is a continuous supply for as long as you stay within the unit's rating, a compact footprint on the wall, and a lifespan that can be nearly double that of a typical tank. Homeowners in Yakima, WA often choose tankless during a replacement or remodel for exactly those reasons.

Most tankless complaints trace back to poor sizing. A unit rated for plenty of flow on a spring day can struggle in winter, when incoming water is much colder and the heater has to raise it far more. So we size for your coldest months and your busiest moments, counting the fixtures that run together. We also confirm the gas line and meter can feed a burner that may be rated near 199,000 BTU, choose the correct venting for condensing or non-condensing equipment, and plan a condensate drain with a neutralizer where needed. For electric tankless, we check the panel first, because whole-home models can outstrip an older electrical service.

Every tankless install from Yakima Water Heater includes isolation service valves so future descaling takes minutes instead of hours, proper gas piping with a drip leg, correctly pitched and terminated venting, and a pressure relief valve piped to a safe location. If your water is hard, we talk through scale protection, since mineral buildup is what shortens tankless life most. We can add recirculation for faster hot water at distant bathrooms, set the temperature, handle permits where required, and walk you through maintenance before we leave. Symptoms That Mean It's Time to Call

Catching a symptom early is often the difference between a quick part swap and a tank that gives out on the garage floor. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Energy bills that reflect a tank heating water around the clock
  • Interest in a longer-lasting heater with replaceable parts
  • A growing household or new bathroom adding hot water demand
  • A remodel that would benefit from a compact wall-mounted heater
  • Hot water running out during back-to-back showers
  • Construction or remodeling that opens walls and simplifies the install
  • A water heater near the end of its service life, making this a natural time to switch
  • An addition or guest suite that is a long run from the main heater

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

Buying the right unit is the easy part. The other half is making sure your home can support it. Gas tankless burners can fire at close to 199,000 BTU, so the size of the gas line, the capacity of the meter, and the pressure at high fire all have to check out. Vent materials, lengths, and terminations are set by the manual and the code, and condensing units add a condensate drain that should be neutralized. Electric whole-home units can pull more current than an older panel can deliver. Miss one of these, and the unit underperforms or becomes unsafe.

Our technicians handle the full picture. We size the unit by flow rate and winter temperature rise instead of guessing, verify gas supply at full fire, and install venting and condensate lines exactly to specification. Every install gets isolation valves for easy descaling and a properly piped relief valve. We also give honest advice about whether tankless suits your home at all. For some households, a high-efficiency tank or a heat pump water heater makes more sense, and you will hear that from us before you spend money on a system that does not match how you live.

Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Here's where to begin:

  • If the unit shows a recurring code, note it and have it checked
  • Check the vent cap outside for nests, debris, or buildup
  • Flush the unit with a descaling solution annually to keep scale off the heat exchanger
  • Treating hard water protects the heat exchanger from mineral buildup
  • Do not cut power to a unit in a cold space, since its freeze protection needs electricity
  • Set the temperature around 120F for safety and efficiency
  • Check and rinse the small filter screen at the cold inlet periodically
