Clear Pricing

Honest, Upfront Pricing

Before we touch a tool, we find out exactly what's wrong. Our technicians look at your heater's age and gallon capacity, the burner or elements, the relief valve, the venting, and any rust or seepage around the fittings. After that you get a straightforward recommendation and the complete price up front, whether the job is replacing a thermocouple or installing a new heater. No hidden fees, no last-minute add-ons.

A lot of water heater failures send small signals well before the tank gives out. Watch for discolored or rusty hot water, a rumbling tank, a sulfur smell from the hot tap, or a hot water supply that runs out faster than before. Behind those symptoms are usually heavy sediment, a depleted anode rod, or a worn dip tube. A quick inspection at the first sign often costs far less than mopping up after a ruptured tank.

Not every visit is about a breakdown. Plenty of homeowners call us to evaluate an aging heater before it fails, and it's a smart move. With a typical tank lifespan of 8 to 12 years (tankless units often run 20 years with annual descaling), replacing on your own schedule beats a rushed swap after a leak. We'll give you a straight answer on how much life your unit has left.

Annual Flushing

Water Heater Maintenance That Adds Years

Routine maintenance is the most reliable way to keep hot water flowing all year. Regular service prevents failures before they start, flushes out mineral sediment, and catches small faults while they're still cheap to fix. At Yakima Water Heater, every maintenance visit is built around your specific heater, covering a full tank flush, an anode rod inspection, a test of the temperature and pressure relief valve, a look at the burner and flue on gas models, element and thermostat testing on electric units, and a temperature check at the tap.

No two maintenance plans look exactly alike. An older tank needs a closer look than a new heater, a tankless unit on hard water calls for annual descaling to protect its heat exchanger clear, and a large family cycles the tank far harder than a couple does. We set up each visit around your equipment and water, so every tune-up keeps the unit efficient and protects the equipment you've already paid for.

Think of maintenance as protection for equipment you've already paid for. Each visit keeps the tank clear of sediment, the anode rod doing its job, and the relief valve in working order, and that lowers what you pay to heat water while easing the strain on the burner or elements. For most families, the plan costs far less than an early replacement.

Right-Sized Hot Water

Hot Water Sized to Your Family's Demand

Bigger isn't always better when it comes to water heaters. What matters for a tank is the first-hour rating, how many gallons of hot water it can deliver in one busy hour, while for a tankless unit it's the flow rate it can sustain when the incoming water is cold. That's why we never just copy the size of the old unit. We look at how many people live in the home, how many bathrooms you have, plus large draws such as a jetted tub or high-capacity washer.

We walk you through the options for every home, especially growing families with rising hot water needs. A 40-gallon tank that served a couple rarely keeps up once kids are showering every morning, and a tankless water heater can deliver continuous hot water as long as it's sized for the flow. If you're weighing tank against tankless, or gas against a heat pump model, our technician will lay out the honest trade-offs: installed price, operating cost, venting and gas line sizing, electrical capacity, and space, so you can pick what fits your home and budget.

Repairs We Handle

The Water Heater Repairs Local Homes Need Most

A cold tap where hot water should be, a burner that refuses to light, a tank full of sediment, a weeping relief valve: each of these problems has its own cause and needs a different repair. Yakima Water Heater tracks down the exact fault before replacing anything, whether it's an electric tank with a burned-out element, a thermocouple that no longer holds the pilot, a broken dip tube sending cold water straight to the tap, or a tankless unit flashing error codes.

Some problems are mostly a nuisance, while others pose real risks. A burned-out element simply leaves you with lukewarm water, but a relief valve that drips or discharges is a warning that pressure or temperature in the tank is running too high, and a thermostat set above 120°F can burn a child in seconds. A gas smell near the heater means leave the house first and call the gas utility from outside, then call us. We match the fix to the failure: elements and thermostats on electric tanks, thermocouples, flame sensors, and gas valves on gas units, a thermal expansion tank where water pressure is climbing, and leak repair on fittings, drain valves, and relief lines.
