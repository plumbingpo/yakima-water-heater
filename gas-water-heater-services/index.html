Most gas water heaters in Yakima, WA work the same basic way. Gas enters through a control valve that doubles as the thermostat and feeds a burner beneath the tank, and the hot exhaust travels up a flue through the middle of the tank and out through the vent. A thermocouple, or a flame sensor on newer electronic models, tells the valve a flame is present; without that signal, the valve closes for safety. The design is dependable, but it has a few predictable weak spots. Thermocouples wear out, pilot tubes and burner ports clog, the flame arrestor screen at the base packs up with lint and dust, control valves fail, and vents lose their draft.

Every gas water heater call starts with safety and then moves to testing. If you ever smell gas, leave the home first, call the gas utility from outside, and do not operate switches or try to relight anything. Once the area is safe, our Yakima technicians work through the heater systematically: millivolt readings on the thermocouple, a close look at the pilot and main burner flames, gas pressure at the control valve, a draft test to make sure exhaust is not spilling back, and a CO check around the appliance. Scorch marks at the burner access, rust flakes on the burner, and a lazy yellow flame each point to a different problem, and we chase down the right one.

The fix is usually straightforward once the cause is known. A new thermocouple or flame sensor, a cleaned pilot tube and burner, a cleared flame arrestor screen, or a replacement gas control valve gets most heaters running again the same day. Vent problems, like a disconnected connector or a flue pipe without enough rise, are corrected on the spot. If sediment is rumbling in the tank, we flush it, and if the anode rod is spent, we can replace it. When replacement is the smarter move, we install new gas heaters sized to your household's first-hour demand, with proper atmospheric, power vent, or direct vent venting, a drip leg on the gas supply, and the expansion tank, drain pan, and strapping your local code calls for. When to Call for Gas Water Heater Services

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • A faint gas or rotten-egg smell near the heater (leave and call the gas utility)
  • A yellow, lazy, or sooty burner flame instead of a crisp blue one
  • Water pooling around the base of the heater
  • Loud rumbling from the bottom of the tank
  • A silent heater with no flame and no hot water
  • Showers that go cold sooner than they did a year ago
  • Exhaust odor, rust, or moisture around the draft hood and vent pipe
  • Pilot outages that keep coming back after a relight

The Benefits of Professional Gas Water Heater Services

Gas water heaters are among the riskiest appliances to work on without training, because the biggest hazards are invisible. A gas connection that is not quite tight can leak slowly for days. A vent connector that has lost its slope, or a flue partly blocked by debris, can let carbon monoxide spill into the house every time the burner fires, with no smell to warn you. Gas valve and heater replacements also typically require a permit and an inspection. Relighting a pilot by following the label is something most homeowners can do; almost everything past that deserves a technician.

The value of calling a pro is certainty. We test instead of guessing, from thermocouple millivolt readings and burner flame checks to gas pressure at the valve and a draft test at the hood. We leak-test every connection, confirm the vent is carrying exhaust out of the house, and check CO before we leave. And because some gas heaters are not worth another repair, we give you a straight answer on repair versus replacement first, then handle either one with correct venting, gas piping, and the code items your installation requires.

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Install carbon monoxide alarms on every level of the home and test them monthly
  • Watch for a loose, rusted, or sagging vent pipe above the heater
  • Check the anode rod every three to five years, since it protects the tank from corrosion
  • Give the heater breathing room and keep flammables well away from it
  • Keep the air intake at the bottom of the heater free of lint and dust
  • Flush the tank on a regular schedule so sediment never bakes onto the bottom
  • Keep the thermostat around 120 degrees to save energy and reduce scalding risk
