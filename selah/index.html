Two common situations call for a quick step from you before our technician gets there. The second is a relief valve that keeps releasing water or a tank that makes loud banging noises: both can mean excess temperature or pressure, so shut the heater down and stay clear of the discharge line. Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Selah

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, lift the T&P valve lever once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which reduces the risk of scalding and cuts down on scale. Tankless owners should plan on descaling every year in hard-water areas.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The serial number on the label usually includes the month and year it was built, and its age tells you a lot. Most tank heaters reach 8 to 12 years, heat pump models 10 to 15, and regularly descaled tankless units around 20. If your heater is past that range and is also showing rusty water, popping sounds, or moisture at the base, it's usually due for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than an emergency replacement after a leak.

Left alone, a small problem tends to grow. Take a spent anode rod: replacing it is a short, inexpensive repair, but once it's gone, the water starts eating the tank steel, and suddenly a minor fix becomes a full replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to save our number in their phone and reach out at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and prevents a surprise cleanup.

What Goes Wrong With Local Water Heaters

Every type of water heater fails in its own way, and we work on all of them around Selah. Gas tank heaters usually give trouble through thermocouples and flame sensors, dirty burners, and draft problems in the flue. Electric units burn out heating elements and thermostats, especially when sediment buries the lower element. Tankless units throw error codes for scale in the heat exchanger, ignition failures, and flow sensor problems, most of which trace back to skipped descaling. Heat pump water heaters need clean air filters and a clear condensate drain, and they struggle in cramped closets. We service all four types regularly and stock the common parts to finish most repairs in one trip.
