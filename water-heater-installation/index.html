Why Hire a Professional for Water Heater Installation?

Installing a water heater from scratch pulls in gas, venting, electrical, and plumbing work all at once. Gas lines must be sized for the total load on the system and tested for leaks. Vents must meet the manufacturer's limits on length, diameter, slope, and clearances, because a bad vent can put carbon monoxide into living space. Electric, tankless, and heat pump heaters need circuits matched to their load, which can exceed what an older panel offers. On the plumbing side, the relief valve needs the right rating and a full-size discharge line, and depending on location, code may require an expansion tank, a drain pan, seismic straps, or a permit and inspection.

Professional installation gets every piece right the first time. Our experienced technicians size the heater to your demand, calculate the gas line, plan the vent route, confirm electrical capacity, and install the code items your location requires. Leaks, draft, and carbon monoxide are tested, and the heater is left at a safe temperature with the paperwork handled. That is the difference between a heater that simply works for years and one that fails inspection or backdrafts on the first cold morning. How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • A periodic checkup with an anode inspection keeps a new tank healthy for years
  • Drain a few gallons from a new tank twice a year to control sediment
  • Check the drain pan and relief valve discharge line now and then for signs of water
  • Nothing flammable belongs near a gas burner or its air supply
  • Do not box the heater in; leave room for service and airflow
  • Descale tankless units once a year, or more often with hard water
  • Keep the temperature around 120 degrees; hotter settings raise the risk of scalding
